🍬 Candy #002 — Cosmic Scale

the Cosmic Calendar

The universe is 13.8 billion years old. That number is so large it's essentially meaningless to our brains. So let's fix that. Compress all of time into a single calendar year. January 1st is the Big Bang. December 31st, 11:59:59 PM is right now.

Every second of this cosmic year equals 437 years of real time. Every day equals 37.8 million years. Scroll through the year and find out where everything — dinosaurs, humans, all of recorded history — falls.

✦ Cosmic Era
The Big Bang
13.8 billion years ago
Everything begins. An infinitely hot, infinitely dense singularity expands outward in an event we call the Big Bang — not an explosion into space, but the creation of space itself. In the first fraction of a second, the four fundamental forces separate. Quarks form. Then protons and neutrons.
The entire observable universe was once smaller than a proton.
Jan 10
✦ Cosmic Era
First Stars Ignite
13.4 billion years ago
The universe cools enough for hydrogen gas to collapse under gravity. The first stars — massive, hot, brilliant — blaze to life. These Population III stars are unlike any stars that exist today: hundreds of times more massive than our Sun, burning fast and dying violently as supernovae.
These first stars forged the first heavy elements — carbon, oxygen, iron — seeding the universe with the ingredients for everything that follows.
Jan 22
✦ Cosmic Era
First Galaxies Form
13 billion years ago
Gravity pulls stars into vast collections — the first galaxies. They are smaller and more chaotic than today's grand spirals, merging and colliding constantly. The Milky Way begins assembling from smaller proto-galactic fragments.

✦ Cosmic Era
The Milky Way Takes Shape
12–11 billion years ago
Our galaxy grows through mergers and accretion into something resembling its current spiral form. Billions of stars are born and die during this period, each generation enriching the galaxy with heavier elements.
May
✦ Cosmic Era
Peak Star Formation
~9 billion years ago
The universe reaches its most productive era — stars are forming at roughly 10 times today's rate across all galaxies. This is the universe's golden age of creation.
The universe has been slowly "winding down" its star formation ever since — we live in the universe's middle age.

Sep 2
◈ Geological Era
Our Solar System Is Born
4.6 billion years ago
A cloud of gas and dust — enriched by countless generations of dead stars — collapses under its own gravity. The center ignites into our Sun. The remaining material flattens into a disk and clumps together to form the planets. Earth is among them.
The iron in your blood was forged in a star that died before our Sun was born.
Sep 6
◈ Geological Era
Earth's Oceans Form
4.4 billion years ago
A Mars-sized body collides with early Earth, ejecting material that forms the Moon. Earth cools. Water arrives — delivered by comets and asteroids — and the first oceans cover the surface. The stage is set.
Sep 21
❧ Life Era
First Life on Earth
3.8 billion years ago
In the oceans — possibly near hydrothermal vents — chemistry crosses a threshold and becomes biology. The first single-celled organisms appear. They are simple, but they are alive. Everything that has ever lived on Earth descends from these microscopic pioneers.
Life appeared on Earth almost as soon as conditions allowed. It may not be rare at all.

Oct 2
❧ Life Era
Photosynthesis — Earth Gets Oxygen
2.7 billion years ago
Cyanobacteria evolve the ability to use sunlight to make energy, releasing oxygen as a byproduct. Over millions of years they transform Earth's atmosphere in what's called the Great Oxidation Event — poisoning most life that existed, but opening the door for complex life to follow.
Oct 9
❧ Life Era
First Complex Cells
2.1 billion years ago
One cell engulfs another — and instead of digesting it, keeps it alive. This symbiotic merger creates the first eukaryotic cell, with a nucleus and specialized organelles. Every complex life form on Earth — plants, animals, fungi — descends from this moment.
Oct 31
❧ Life Era
First Multicellular Life
1.2 billion years ago
Cells begin cooperating — staying together and specializing rather than living alone. Multicellular life emerges. This Halloween milestone marks the beginning of the long road to visible, complex organisms.

Nov 15
◉ Animal Era
The Cambrian Explosion
540 million years ago
In a geologically brief window, almost every major animal body plan appears in the fossil record. Eyes, limbs, shells, mouths — the blueprint for all complex animal life is written in this extraordinary burst of evolution. The oceans suddenly teem with visible, complex creatures.
The Cambrian Explosion lasted about 20 million years — less than one cosmic calendar day.
Nov 21
◉ Animal Era
Life Conquers Land
430 million years ago
Plants venture onto land first, followed by insects, then amphibians. Earth's empty continents transform from bare rock into lush ecosystems. The atmosphere fills with oxygen from land plants — the air becomes breathable for future creatures.
Nov 28
◉ Animal Era
Dinosaurs Arise
230 million years ago
Following the devastating Permian–Triassic extinction, dinosaurs emerge and rapidly become the dominant vertebrates on land. They will reign for 165 million years — more than 4 cosmic calendar days.

🗓 We have arrived at December — the final month of the cosmic year. All of recorded human history, every empire, every invention, every war, every discovery — it all fits inside the last 14 seconds of December 31st. Keep scrolling.
Dec 5
◉ Animal Era
Asteroid Wipes Out the Dinosaurs
66 million years ago
A 10-km asteroid strikes the Yucatán Peninsula. The impact triggers fires, a "nuclear winter," and the 5th mass extinction — killing 75% of all species, including all non-avian dinosaurs. The age of mammals begins. Small, furry survivors inherit the Earth.
Birds are living dinosaurs. When you watch a pigeon, you're watching a dinosaur that survived the apocalypse.
Dec 28
◌ Human Era
Our Ancestors Walk Upright
3–4 million years ago
Early hominids — including Australopithecus — begin walking on two legs across the African savanna. Their hands are freed. Their brains begin a long, slow expansion. The human story has begun — though it will be a very long time before anything resembling civilization appears.

~10:30 PM
◌ Human Era
Homo Sapiens Appear
200,000 years ago
Anatomically modern humans evolve in Africa. We are here. But the entire span of our species so far — 200,000 years of Homo sapiens — occupies only the last 26 minutes of this cosmic year.
Dec 31
~11:48 PM
◌ Human Era
Humans Nearly Go Extinct
70,000 years ago
The Toba supervolcano erupts — the largest eruption in the last 2 million years. It triggers a decade-long volcanic winter and reduces the human population to perhaps just a few thousand individuals. Every person alive today descends from that tiny group of survivors.
Dec 31
~11:58 PM
◆ Recorded History
Agriculture — Civilization Begins
12,000 years ago
Humans in the Fertile Crescent begin domesticating plants and animals. Instead of following food, they grow it. Settlements form. Populations grow. Cities, writing, governments, and trade networks follow. Everything we call "civilization" springs from this moment.
We have been farming for less than 1 cosmic second.
The Last 14 Seconds

All of recorded human history — every empire, philosopher, artist, scientist, war, and discovery — fits inside the final 14 seconds of December 31st. Here is what happened.

11:59:46 PM — 5,000 years ago
Ancient Egypt
Pyramids of Giza built
The Great Pyramid is constructed. The pharaohs rule. Writing, mathematics, and astronomy flourish along the Nile.
11:59:51 PM — 2,500 years ago
The Golden Age
Greece, Rome, Buddha, Confucius
Socrates, Plato, Aristotle philosophize. The Roman Republic rises. Buddha teaches in India. Confucius in China. An extraordinary flowering of human thought — all in one second.
11:59:54 PM — 1,000 years ago
Medieval World
Crusades, Islamic Golden Age
The Islamic Golden Age preserves and advances mathematics and science. Leif Erikson reaches North America. The first universities appear in Europe.
11:59:57 PM — 500 years ago
Renaissance & Revolution
Da Vinci, Columbus, Copernicus
Columbus reaches the Americas. Copernicus overturns the geocentric universe. Da Vinci sketches flying machines. The printing press spreads ideas faster than ever before.
11:59:59 PM — 225 years ago
Industrial Revolution
Steam, electricity, science
Steam engines, electricity, and the scientific method transform human civilization faster than any previous era. The population begins its exponential climb.

Space travel. The internet. Artificial intelligence. 8 billion humans on a pale blue dot, 26,000 light-years from the center of a galaxy, in the final fraction of a second of a cosmic year.

13.8 billion years of cosmic evolution produced stars, galaxies, planets, life, complexity, and eventually — you. Reading this. Right now. In the last microsecond of the cosmic year. That is not nothing. That is everything.
